# IncidentRelay commercial licensing

IncidentRelay is available under the Elastic License 2.0 (ELv2) for uses
permitted by that license. Separate commercial licensing is available for
organizations that need additional rights.

## Hosted and managed services

If your organization wants to provide IncidentRelay, or a service exposing a
substantial set of IncidentRelay features or functionality, to third parties as
a hosted or managed service, obtain a commercial license before launching that
offering.

Commercial licensing can cover use cases such as:

- SaaS and IncidentRelay-as-a-Service;
- managed or multi-tenant IncidentRelay hosting;
- white-label hosted services;
- OEM or embedded commercial distribution;
- negotiated redistribution rights;
- commercial branding arrangements;
- enterprise support, warranty, indemnity, or other negotiated terms.

## No commercial rights are granted by this file

This document describes the availability of commercial licensing. It is not a
commercial license grant and does not itself give any organization additional
rights beyond the license that already applies to the software.

Additional commercial rights exist only after the copyright holder and the
customer enter into a separate written agreement that expressly grants those
rights.

## Expected commercial agreement

A signed commercial agreement should normally identify at least:

- the licensor's legal name and address;
- the customer's legal name and address;
- the IncidentRelay software and versions covered;
- whether SaaS, hosted, managed, OEM, white-label, or redistribution rights are
  granted;
- permitted production environments, customers, tenants, or usage limits, if
  applicable;
- fees and payment terms;
- agreement term and renewal terms;
- support and service obligations, if any;
- confidentiality terms, if any;
- intellectual-property ownership;
- warranty, liability, and indemnity terms;
- termination and post-termination rights;
- governing law and dispute-resolution terms.

## Requesting a license

Contact the IncidentRelay copyright holder through the project repository with:

1. your organization's legal name;
2. a short description of the product or service;
3. whether customers will access the IncidentRelay UI, APIs, or workflows;
4. whether the service is single-tenant or multi-tenant;
5. expected number of customers or deployments;
6. whether you need white-label, OEM, redistribution, support, or indemnity
   rights.

The copyright holder can then provide commercial terms appropriate for that
use case.

## Legal identity

A repository username or project name should not be used as a substitute for
the licensor's legal identity in a signed commercial agreement. Before signing
a commercial agreement, replace project-only identifiers with the correct legal
name, address, and other required contracting details.
